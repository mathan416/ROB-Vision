INSIDE THE SIGNAL / THE EXPERIMENTS / THE FRAME LINK

HOW BUDDY
HEARS THE GAME.

The games still speak in flashes. R.O.B. Vision listens where every rendered NES frame is available, then gives Buddy one complete move at a time.

THE CURRENT SYSTEM

FROM A FLASH
TO A MOVE.

The console runs the original game in FCEUmm or Nestopia. A small libretro wrapper observes the rendered light frames, while normal video continues to your game screen.

01 / GAMENES FRAMESGyromite or Stack-Up renders a light message.
02 / CONSOLEFRAME LINKThe wrapper and receiver check a complete, game-specific word.
03 / UNO QVIRTUAL MODELBuddy moves only when the command is valid for his pose and pieces.
04 / BROWSERLIVE STAGEMission shows the robot, accessories, and Activity Feed.

EXACT COMMANDS

A movement is a 13-frame dark/green word. The receiver requires consecutive frames and an exact word allowed for the selected game. Missing or ambiguous frames do not become guessed moves.

A RETURN PATH

Gyromite also needs Buddy to affect the game. The UNO Q's red and blue pad states return through a virtual Controller 2 on the console, letting a gyro lower a matching gate.

THE QUESTION THAT CHANGED THE DESIGN

WHY NOT A CAMERA?

That was the original plan. A Kiyo Pro watched a modern LCD while an UNO Q sampled the flashes. The historical tests taught us exactly where commands were getting lost.

~60

FRAMES PER SECOND

Live Kiyo Pro traces delivered about 60 fps in the tested 640×480 MJPEG setup. Test-mode fields were visible, but short movement cells were unreliable.

72

ROW REGIONS

A 1080p study checked three image strips across 24 bands. None recovered the five source-aligned commands that whole-frame sampling missed.

13

FRAMES PER WORD

RetroArch recordings showed full game words even when the camera trace did not. The signal existed before the LCD and camera path.

The measured result: this camera, display, and capture setup could recognise Test light and occasional movement, but it did not deliver dependable autonomous play. These tests do not claim that every possible camera setup would fail.

THE ENGINEERING JOURNEY

KEEP THE LIGHT.
CHANGE THE VANTAGE.

01

READ THE ORIGINAL WORDS

Analysis identified command patterns, each game's distinct Up and Down codes, and separate Test and ready-light signals.

02

TEST THE OPTICAL ROUTE

Synthetic traces, live Gyromite and Stack-Up sessions, and source-aligned Kiyo Pro row captures showed that status fields survived more easily than short command cells. The decoder rejected incomplete or ambiguous words.

03

MOVE TO RENDERED FRAMES

The libretro wrapper now classifies each rendered NES frame before display. The receiver checks the sender and ROM identity, decodes a complete word, and sends the action to the paired UNO Q.

04

PROVE BOTH CORES

Live FCEUmm Direct-mode runs delivered all six movement types in both games. Nestopia also delivered movement and required an explicit Player 2 gamepad selection for Gyromite's gate return.

WHAT THE TESTS SHOW

A SIGNAL BUDDY
CAN FOLLOW.

The tests trace complete commands from rendered game frames through the console link to Buddy's animation.

VERIFIED PATHS

The frame link works with RetroPie and Batocera through FCEUmm and Nestopia wrappers. On Recalbox 10.1.1, both Buddy games launched through FCEUmm and reported their sessions to the UNO Q. Buddy follows the rendered command words and shows the virtual game table in a browser.
