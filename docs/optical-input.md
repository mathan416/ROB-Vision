# Optical game input: camera watching a modern display

## Current implementation

The UNO Q's optional OpenCV capture path watches the cabinet's LCD/OLED game display. It requests 60 fps, timestamps delivered frames, samples normalized green dominance from the central 60% rectangle by default, and passes samples to `controller/optical.py`. Setup shows a low-rate camera preview with the sampling rectangle, measured fps, and signal level. A CLI `--camera-roi x,y,width,height` can change the crop. The diagnostic trace retains the sampled signal and its timestamps. Earlier wide-frame and horizontal-band measurements were research tools; capture no longer computes them on every frame. The browser does not offer a draggable crop or automatic display locator.

The decoder uses the ROM-derived `000101w1x1y1z` command family and a 60 Hz source-cell model. Six commands map to left, right, up, down, open, and close; Gyromite moves two levels vertically while Stack-Up moves one. Gyromite's sustained green field or Stack-Up's alternating green/white Test signal blinks R.O.B.'s red status light, and the ready-light command glows steadily without moving pieces. Only complete, unambiguous transmissions cause one model action. Partial or ambiguous samples are rejected. Separate repeated transmissions remain valid commands.

On the Kiyo Pro, the app defaults to 640×480 MJPEG. A device-local `data/camera-mode` file can select `yuyv720` for a controlled 1280×720 uncompressed experiment; an absent or unrecognized value selects `mjpg480`. Changing resolution changes the normalized position of the screen, so the saved `data/camera-roi` must be recalibrated before comparing modes. The UNO Q currently uses the default mode. The 720p experiment delivered about 60 fps but missed two of three observed Stack-Up commands, so it is not a reliability upgrade by itself.

## Measured boundary

Synthetic stress tests with timing jitter at 60 fps decoded 667 of 900 valid command traces and rejected 233; none in that run became a wrong command. At 30 fps, all 900 were rejected. Ten further 900-trace seeds also produced zero wrong commands. These results show that conservative rejection works, but they do **not** establish reliable play. The attached Kiyo Pro delivered about 60 fps at 640×480, and both games' Test modes were recognized with a tight crop. Live Stack-Up RIGHT and DOWN flashes each decoded and moved the virtual R.O.B., but repeated commands were missed. A nominal 60 fps setting does not guarantee alignment with one-frame flashes. Frame drops, rolling shutter, display persistence, and video filters can change the signal.

After the camera was moved closer and aimed straight-on, the small dark crop and a wide average rose and fell on the same camera frames. The wide average did not recover the missing command cells. Twelve horizontal band measurements did reveal different green/dark levels at the top and bottom of individual frames, consistent with scan timing. One band decoded an UP signal in offline replay, but three further UP/RIGHT cycles yielded no complete decoder result from any band. UP_STACK (`0001011111010`) and RIGHT (`0001011101010`) differ only at bit 8, counting from zero; a missed green cell at that position can make UP resemble RIGHT. The band data is diagnostic only; no band can command R.O.B. until repeated tests establish correct identity and a low false-trigger rate.

## Kiyo Pro opportunities (25 September 2026)

Razer identifies the Kiyo Pro sensor as Sony IMX327 STARVIS and specifies USB 3, 1080p60, 720p60, and an HDR mode capped at 30 fps. Its USB 3 modes include uncompressed YUY2 and NV12. Sony's IMX327 information describes variable electronic shutter integration and full-pixel scan at up to 1080p60. On the attached UNO Q, V4L2 exposes MJPEG, YUYV, NV12, and H264 up to 60 fps; the USB link is running at SuperSpeed. The Linux UVC metadata node is `/dev/video3`, with `UVCH` format. A 30-buffer local capture verified host timestamps, USB frame numbers, and camera PTS/SCR fields on this camera.

Raw NV12 at 1920×1080 can be read at about 60 delivered fps when OpenCV color conversion is disabled. A whole-frame conversion to BGR on the UNO Q achieved only about 36 fps. Raw luma and chroma measurements from selected rows therefore retain full camera throughput without processing every color pixel. The first vertical strip missed the active flash area; two later captures sampled three correct strips and 24 bands per strip while independently controlled Stack-Up Direct moves were recorded by RetroArch.

With the ordinary view, only two of four source-recorded commands were exact in the camera average. The third captured an extra green bit and the fourth had two changed bits. Checking all 72 narrow row regions recovered neither rejected command. Digital zoom and tilt made the game display occupy most of the camera image; only one of four source-recorded commands was exact in that framing, and none of the row regions recovered the other three. The scan-line approach did not improve this setup. These measurements do not establish that the camera has a useful rolling-shutter timing offset for this display. They do establish that 1080p raw capture and chroma sampling are feasible on the UNO Q.

The Kiyo's `/dev/video3` metadata stream was also captured concurrently with the normal preview: 30 `UVCH` blocks, each containing a host timestamp, USB start-of-frame number, and camera PTS/SCR fields. These timing fields are real and could replace the current timestamp taken *after* `capture.read()`, but cannot recreate a flash that never reached a captured image. Keep the exact-pattern, unambiguous-command requirement; a guessed missing bit can turn Stack-Up UP into RIGHT. The [source-aligned row test](KIYO_PRO_ROW_TEST_2026-09-25.md) records the method and results.

The original R.O.B. received light continuously with a photosensor looking at a CRT. The camera path instead samples an LCD/OLED through a USB capture pipeline. Higher image resolution alone does not close that timing gap. The Kiyo's short manual exposure (currently 1 ms) and uncompressed row access are the capabilities worth testing. A trial at the shortest exposed 0.3 ms setting produced an overexposed preview and about 30 delivered fps on this setup, so it was reverted.

Sources: [Razer Kiyo Pro support](https://mysupport.razer.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/4104/kw/camera%20app), [Razer's USB format description](https://www.razer.com/newsroom/product-news/show-you-mean-business-with-the-outstanding-video-quality-of-the-new-razer-kiyo-pro-webcam), [Sony IMX327 product information](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/pdf/p-12_IMX327LQR_LQR1_Flyer02.pdf), and [Linux UVC metadata format](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/userspace-api/media/v4l/metafmt-uvc.html).

## Commissioning test

1. Attach the intended camera, point it at the game's flash region, and use Setup's **Start Camera Check**. Check the delivered fps and green sample rectangle.
2. In each game's Test mode, choose **Watch Test Signal**. Confirm the Test signal blinks the virtual red light without causing movement. Repeat under normal room lighting and beside the UNO Q matrix.
3. Capture each of the six commands many times in both games, plus ready signals and idle gameplay. Compare accepted/rejected actions with what the game actually displayed. Include Stack-Up Memory speed and Bingo's no-command simultaneous row/column case.
4. Change display scaling, exposure, glare, and camera aim slightly. Record measured fps, dropped frames, decode counts, and false triggers. A proposed project target is at least 99 correct in 100 transmissions per command with zero idle false triggers; it has **not** been met.
5. For RetroPie play, use the installed FCEUmm game-frame link and verify each movement against the game display. The camera path remains an independent experiment for original hardware or game hosts without the frame link.

Nintendo's [photosensing patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4815733A/en) and the [historical manual](HISTORICAL_MANUAL_NOTES.md) explain the original CRT light mechanism. They do not prove a modern camera/display combination will work. The [ROM analysis](ROM_SIGNAL_ANALYSIS.md) gives the exact patterns; the [synthetic test report](UNATTENDED_TEST_REPORT_2026-09-24.md) and [live optical test report](LIVE_OPTICAL_TEST_2026-09-25.md) separate simulation from hardware results.
